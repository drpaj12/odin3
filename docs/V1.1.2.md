## 1. Current state (file:line, v1.1.1 main `0b90fff9`)

### 1.1 What a record holds

- A record has `kind` (SOURCE, IMPORTED, DERIVED), `run`, `op`, `locs`, `ast`, `hier`, `parents`
  (`src/ir/prov.h:120`). Records are immutable and hash-consed; SOURCE and IMPORTED identity is
  every field except `op`, DERIVED identity includes `run` and `op` (`src/ir/prov.h:80-86`,
  `docs/IR.md` IR-12, IR-13). Record IDs grow in creation order, so every parent is older than its
  child (`src/ir/prov.h:23-26`). A repeated parent in a DERIVED record is kept once
  (`src/ir/prov.h:99-100`).
- The elaborator makes SOURCE records per AST node: wire to its declarator, cell to the expression
  that made it, `$proc` to its `ALWAYS`, each action, case and switch to its statement
  (`docs/specs/2026-10-11-2D-elaboration-design.md` section 10, EL-45; code
  `src/frontends/elab/elab2_wires.c:94` `prov_for`, `src/frontends/elab/pg.c:474` `pg_stmt_prov`,
  `src/frontends/elab/pg_always.c:648`). The record stores the node's own span, the node's ID in
  the elaborated store (`ast`) and `hier`. **When the node sits in an inlined function or task, the
  record also stores the call site as a second location** (`elab2_wires.c:94-101`, the `req->call`
  branch): a SOURCE record with `ast` set and two locations is a call-site record.
- **SOURCE records are flat.** The record of `q <= d` inside `if (en)` inside `always` carries the
  span and `ast` of the assignment only; no parent edge reaches the record of the `if` or of the
  `always` (`src/frontends/elab/pg.c:479-480` makes each record from one node and one scope; SOURCE
  has no parents, `src/ir/prov.h:120-129`). The nesting lives only in the AST, reachable through
  `ast` and `odin3_ast_parent_index_build`, which picks the parent by span containment
  (`src/ast/ast.h:421-428`).
- `hier` holds the specialization name and **named** generate and block scopes only: an unnamed
  scope is skipped (`src/frontends/elab/elab2_wires.c:81`, `rec->name != 0`). So an unnamed `if` or
  `case` is not a level in `hier`; and `hier` is module-relative until `flatten` adds the instance
  path (`docs/IR.md` IR-12, `src/passes/flatten/prov.c:33-62`, `odin3_flatten_derive` at `:65`).
- Restored locations (`read_verilog -netlist`, `read_json`) make a SOURCE record with `ast` 0 and
  up to `ODIN3_HDL_LOC_MAX` (8) locations (`src/frontends/hdl_restore.c:42-43`), plus a DERIVED
  record with the SOURCE and the base record as parents (`:46-48`); BLIF readers make IMPORTED
  records (structural only).

### 1.5 The exact gaps

1. **An unnamed `if` or `case` is not a level.** No SOURCE record points at its parent
   (1.1); `hier` skips unnamed scopes (`elab2_wires.c:81`); a `proc` mux points at the switch and
   case records but not at the `always` (`docs/specs/2026-10-11-2E-proc-design.md:898-906`).
2. **Only the first SOURCE leaf is printed.** Names, the map entry and the timing join use
   `locs[0]` of one leaf (`prov_name.c:9-27`); the others exist only via `--src-attr` (BLIF PLAIN),
   JSON `hdl`, Verilog `src`, capped at 8.
3. **Joins are not labelled.** Merge, CSE and ABC regions make multi-parent DERIVED records
   (1.2) but nothing says that distinct HDL constructs were blended, which pass blended them, or
   which constructs.
4. **No operation class.** Refine, rewrite and coarsen are not recorded and cannot be guessed (1.2).
5. **ABC bulk fallback.** Over budget, one record covers the whole run and every new LUT carries
   it (`src/passes/abc/prov.h:66-75`): a LUT then has every cell of the run as an ancestor. Norns
   sees this as "its budget makes whole runs share one record" (`scripts/build_words.py:18`).
6. **No records after Odin III.** BLE, CLB and site are not in the IR or the BLIF; V1.1.1's
   attribution works at atom level only.
7. **Records and the AST layer are not exported.** Neither the chain, nor `hier` of a record, nor
   the run and op, nor the AST, reaches a file or the Python API; the AST is gone by write time.
8. **Netlist-derived records have no AST** (`hdl_restore.c:42-43`, IMPORTED).
9. **Map read-back is partial.** Only the seven-field lines are read back (1.3).

## 2. Level vocabulary

One ordered list, coarse to fine, for constructs and objects. A level is the granularity of an
object or a construct. The names are exact and appear in the schema. There is no `design` level
(no record or AST node sits above a `module`) and no `bit` level (a bit is a position inside a word
cell, and `lower` makes one record per word cell, shared by its gates, `connect.c:20-35`).

| Level | Kind of thing | Source of the node |
|---|---|---|
| `module` | a module specialization | AST `MODULE` |
| `block` | a process, continuous assign, generate construct or subprogram | AST `ALWAYS`, `INITIAL`, `CONT_ASSIGN`, `NET_ASSIGN`, `GENERATE`, `GEN_FOR`, `GEN_IF`, `GEN_CASE`, `GEN_BLOCK`, `FUNCTION_DECL`, `TASK_DECL`, `SPECIFY_BLOCK` |
| `branch` | an if or case arm | AST `CASE_ITEM`; for `IF`, two synthesized arm rows `IF_THEN` and `IF_ELSE` (2.2) |
| `stmt` | a statement, declaration or switch | AST `IF`, `CASE`, assignments, loops, timing statements, blocks, declarations (2.1) |
| `expr` | an expression that made a cell | AST `BINARY`, `UNARY`, `TERNARY`, `SELECT`, `CONCAT`, `CALL`, ... (2.1) |
| `word` | a word-level cell | IR node, granularity `word` (IR-9) |
| `gate` | a bit-level cell (`$_AND_`, ...) | IR node, granularity `bit` (IR-9) |
| `lut` | a `$lut` cell | IR node of type `$lut` |
| `ble` | a basic logic element | VPR `.net` (Task 8) |
| `clb` | a cluster | VPR `.net` (Task 8) |
| `site` | a placed grid location and subtile | VPR `.place` (Task 8) |

Hard and black-box cells keep their IR-9 tag `hard` or `blackbox` at the rank of `word`, with the
cell type in the object's `flags` (`cell=<type>`). `gate`, `lut` and `word` objects are told apart
by the object (granularity tag and cell type), not by the record: a record is shared by the gates
of a word cell, so the **object** carries the level, in the `O` and `N` lines (3.2). A net takes the
level of its driver; a wire and a port bit have level `word`.

**Instance is a separate axis.** An `INSTANCE` node is a sibling of the `ALWAYS` blocks in the parent
module, not their ancestor. The instance is not a rung of the ladder above. It enters the DAG as
flatten's context parent (3.1), and an object's instance path is the sequence of context parents
its walk passes (3.3). In an unflattened hierarchy there is no instance chain: the object lives in
its own module, and its chain stops at that `module`; the instance level appears only once `flatten`
has run. The construct table still holds `INSTANCE` rows (parent: the enclosing module) so that a
context parent resolves to a construct.

### 2.2 The synthesized `IF_THEN` and `IF_ELSE` rows

The arm of an `if` is not an AST node: `IF` has the slots condition, then-statement and optional
else-statement (`src/ast/kinds.h:69`, `SL3(E, S, S_OPT)`). A SOURCE record for a statement in an
arm points at that statement node, not at the arm. The construct table therefore gets a **synthetic
row per arm**, keyed `(run, ast_of_IF, part)` with `part` 1 for the then-arm and 2 for the else-arm
(`part` 0 is the node itself, so the key of every ordinary row is `(run, ast, 0)` and no key
collides with the `IF` row). The arm row's parent is the `IF` row; its span is the arm
statement's span. A statement resolves to its arm by the root-down walk of 4.3: when the walk
descends from an `IF` through child slot 1 or 2 it first emits the arm row, then the child with the
arm row as its parent. A statement that is not a direct child of an `IF` or a `CASE_ITEM` has no arm
row. Rule 12 (4.3) requires every key to be unique.

## 3. Records, joins and the export schema

### 3.1 What a step records: the operation class

The class of an operation is **declared by the pass**, not inferred. A design-global op-class table
(IR addition, Task 2) holds, keyed by `(run, op)`:

- `dir`: `origin` (SOURCE or IMPORTED; implicit, never declared), `refine` (one parent, many
  pieces share the record: `proc`, `lower`, `unmap`), `rewrite` (1:1: `opt` rewrites, `hardmap`,
  `lpmmap`, `dfflibmap`, `submod`, restored locations), `coarsen` (N to 1: merge, CSE, FF merge,
  `abc`, `raise`), `context` (flatten: parent 0 is the data parent, later parents are context, the
  instance record);
- `from` and `to` levels (section 2);
- `bulk`: a flag, set by `abc` for the over-budget record and the record of unanchored objects.

The table is a side table: the provenance record, its identity and its hash-consing are unchanged.
It is stored as runs `{run, first_op, class}`, a new row only when the class changes from the
previous operation of the run, at most 12 bytes per operation and usually a few rows per run
(measured operation counts in section 5.2).

Interface (internal, `src/ir/prov.h`; the public ABI exposes only the result, 4.3):
`odin3_prov_begin_op_class(ctx, const odin3_opclass *cls)` starts the next operation and declares
its class in one call. The undeclared `odin3_prov_begin_op(ctx)` stays for ops that make only SOURCE
or IMPORTED records; **`check` rule 13 fails in Debug when a DERIVED record's `(run, op)` has no
declaration**, and `tools/lint.sh` gets a rule that rejects a call to `odin3_prov_begin_op` outside
`src/ir/` and the readers. The sites that must declare (all from `grep odin3_prov_begin_op src`):

| Site | Class |
|---|---|
| `src/passes/lower/connect.c:29` | refine, `word` to `gate` |
| `src/passes/proc/proc_emit.c:607` | refine, `stmt`/`branch` to `word` (the mux arms are decided in 3.3 and question 5) |
| `src/passes/flatten/expand.c:325` | context |
| `src/passes/opt/kit.c:1057` (`odin3_kit_begin_rewrite`), `:904`, `src/passes/opt/ff.c:789` | rewrite or coarsen by the caller: `odin3_kit_begin_rewrite` takes the class; its 13 users are `dff_gates.c`, `dff.c`, `muxtree.c`, `wreduce.c`, `share.c`, `expr_ffpol.c`, `reduce.c`, `merge.c`, `dff_state.c`, `expr_util.c`, `dff_merge.c`, `ffbits.c` and `src/passes/fsm/map.c` |
| `src/passes/abc/prov.c:449` (`odin3_abc_coarse_record`) | coarsen, `gate` to `lut`, **bulk** (also reached from `sp_coarse`, `splice.c:236-238`, for objects no anchor reaches: the same class) |
| `src/passes/abc/prov.c:482`, `:541` | coarsen, `gate` to `lut` |
| `src/passes/partial_map/hm_kit.c:221`, `src/passes/lpmmap/lm_kit.c:185`, `src/passes/dfflibmap/dfflibmap.c:289` | rewrite, `word`/`gate` to `word` (hard tag) |

At export, every DERIVED record is described by: `step` (pass run name, arguments, run number, op),

### 3.2 The schema, version 2

The map stays one TAB-separated UTF-8 file with LF lines, the escaping of version 1
(`%09 %0A %5C %25`, `%23` for a leading `#` of the first field,
`src/backends/common/name_map.h:1-12`) **plus `%2C` and `%7C`**, because list fields are
comma-separated and `|`-separated and `hier=<text>` (`flags`) can hold `,`, `|` and `"` (a
string-parameter specialization holds `/`, see `flatten/prov.c:30-31`); `%2C` and `%7C` are
escaped in every field of a version 2 line, and the parser unescapes them first. The map has the
same name (`<netlist>.names`, or `--name-map`). It is written only on request (3.5); without the
request the file is version 1 and byte-identical to today. Version 2 begins:

```
# odin3-names 2 hash=sha256 width=10 prov=1 ast=refs
```

`ast=` is `refs`, `constructs`, `full` or `none` (section 4): what the map holds, which is at most
what the read kept. Every later line starts with a one letter type, so a reader ignores a type it
does not know (a located WARNING, as version 1 does for a malformed line) and a version 1 reader
refuses the file at the header (unknown version).

| Type | Fields (after the type letter) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `O` | `module kind short full hier file line recs level` | an object renamed by `hash`: version 1's seven fields unchanged, then `recs` (comma list of record IDs `r<n>`, the naming record first) and `level` (section 2) |
| `N` | `module kind name recs level` | an object that keeps its name (a port bit, a generator) and has records |
| `R` | `id kind level dir step parents origins cons flags` | one provenance record |
| `C` | `id kind level parent file span name` | one construct of the AST layer |
| `F` | `id path` | a file, once, referenced by `R` and `C` as `f<n>` |
| `T` | `id run pass args info` | a pass run, referenced by `R.step` as `t<n>.<op>` |
| `A` | `atom recs` | post-Odin: an atom (BLIF net name as VPR sees it) with its BLE, CLB and site records (Task 8) |

Fields of `R`:

- `id`: `r<n>`, numbered from 1 by a deterministic traversal (objects in the sorted order of the
  `O` and `N` lines, records breadth first, parents before children), so equal designs give equal
  bytes and the numbers do not depend on IR IDs.
- `kind`: `S` (SOURCE), `I` (IMPORTED), `D` (DERIVED).
- `level`: for `D`, the `to` level of its operation's class; for `S`, the level of its construct.
- `dir`: as 3.1.
- `step`: `t<n>.<op>`.
- `parents`: comma list of `r<n>`.
- `origins`: for `S` and `I`, the locations `f<n>:L.C-EL.EC`, `|` separated, at most 8 (the cap of
  `ODIN3_HDL_LOC_MAX`, with `capped` in `flags`), full spans, columns included.
- `cons`: for `S`, the construct `c<n>` (empty when `ast` is 0, or the leaf is restored or
  imported). For a `D` with a join, the reduced set `J` of 3.3.
- `flags`: comma list from `join`, `join=instance`, `lossy=bulk`, `lossy=cap`, `n=<total>`,
  `chain=span`, `capped`, `hier=<text>` (the record's `hier` string, escaped; a record with `hier`
  0 has none), `call=c<n>` (a call-site record, 4.3), `cell=<type>` on hard objects.

Fields of `C`: `id` is `c<n>`; `kind` the AST kind (or `IF_THEN`, `IF_ELSE`, `CALL_SITE`); `level`
as section 2 (`instance` rows have level `instance` and parent: the enclosing module); `parent`
the parent construct or empty for a `module`; `file` `f<n>`; `span` `L.C-EL.EC`; `name` the
construct's name where it has one (module, instance, block label, declarator).

### 3.3 Joins

**Leaves and constructs.** The backward walk from a record `s` follows parents depth-first, each
record once. Two kinds of parent edge are not data and are skipped by the join computation:
parents of a `context` record after the first (flatten's instance record), and nothing else. The
SOURCE leaves reached are classified:

- a leaf with a construct (`ast` != 0 and a row in the table) is a **construct leaf**;
- a leaf without one (restored locations, IMPORTED, `ast` 0) is a **span-only leaf**. Span-only
  leaves are never members of `J` and never make a join (a netlist-restored record carries up to 8
  locations, `hdl_restore.c:42-43`; counting each as a construct would fake joins). The walk records
  their number as `chain=span` on `s`.

**Anchor.** A construct leaf is mapped to its **anchor**: the construct itself if its level is
`stmt` or coarser (`stmt`, `branch`, `block`, `module`), otherwise its nearest ancestor of level
`stmt` or coarser. Distinctness is decided between anchors, never between expressions, so the
`$mul` and `$add` cells of `x = a*b + c` (two `expr` leaves of one statement, neither an ancestor
of the other) have the same anchor and are not a join. For a leaf in an inlined function or task,
the construct is the call-instance row (4.3), so the anchor of the same function body called from
two `always` blocks differs by call site.

**Reduced set.** `J(s)` is the set of anchors of `s`'s construct leaves with every member that is
an ancestor of another member removed. Two anchors `a` and `b` are *distinct* when neither equals
nor is an ancestor of the other. Let `J(p)` be the same set for a parent record `p`.

**Rules.**
- `s` is a **join of distinct constructs** (`flags` has `join`) when its class is `coarsen`,
  `|J(s)| >= 2`, and `J(s)` is not equal to `J(p)` of any single parent `p`: the step itself
  blended constructs; a blend inherited from one parent is not a new join. `cons` lists `J(s)`.
- `s` is a **join of instances** (`join=instance`) when its class is `coarsen` and two construct
  leaves with the **same anchor** are reached through different **context chains**. The context
  chain of a leaf visit is the sequence of context records passed on the walk from `s`
  (flatten's instance record of each nesting level); two instances of one module differ in that
  sequence. This is computed from the record graph, not from `hier` strings (so the named
  generate scopes of one instance do not look like instances), and the instance members never enter
  `J(s)`.
- Two bits of one statement, a statement and its own condition (same anchor), or an `if` and its
  arms, are not a join. `refine`, `rewrite` and `context` records are never joins.
- **Proc mux arms.** A `proc` mux has the switch record and one record per case as parents, so it
  merges several arm statements. Decision (V112-12, question 5): `proc` operations are declared
  `refine`, so the mux is not a join. The arms of one `if` or `case` belong to one `stmt`
  (the switch); a mux is the hardware form of that one statement, and Peter's "joining of ideas" is
  about blending separate constructs. The alternative is `coarsen` with the rule above, which would
  label every mux of a two-arm `if` a join of its two arm statements.
- **One cap rule.** `cons` lists at most 16 members of `J(s)`, in construct-ID order. When
  `|J(s)| > 16` the record has `lossy=cap` and `n=<|J(s)|>`. When the operation was declared `bulk`
  (3.1; the pass says so, not the construct count) the record has `lossy=bulk` and `n=<|J(s)|>`,
  whatever `|J|` is: a legitimately wide cone of a normal operation is not mislabelled bulk, and a
  small bulk run is not missed. Both flags can be set.

The pack and place records of Task 8 use the same rule: a `clb` record whose BLE records have
distinct anchors is a join labelled by the `pack` step.

### 3.4 The registry of levels per step

Section 3.1's table is the registry: each declared class carries its `from`/`to` levels, so the
levels are not derived from the pass name or the cell types. `read_verilog` and the slang reader
are `origin` (levels from the construct kind). `pack` and `place` (Task 8) are declared by the
post-Odin tool into the `T` rows: `pack` coarsen `lut` to `ble` to `clb`, `place` rewrite to `site`.
`names --check` verifies that every `R` has a declared class.

### 3.5 Export options

On `write_blif --vtr`, `write_json` and `write_verilog`, `--prov-map [refs|constructs|full]` makes
the map version 2 with that AST layer; without it the map is version 1 as today.

- `--prov-map` **requires `--name-style hash`** and is an error otherwise (`ODIN3_ERR_INVALID_ARG`,
  "--prov-map needs --name-style hash"): `write_blif` PLAIN, short or provenance names and Verilog
  without hash names carry no map. This is a stated limit (D14 Consequences).
- The mode `--prov-map full` is a request for **at most what the read kept** (4.2): asking for more
  than was kept is an error naming `read_verilog --prov-ast`.
- `odin3-flow` passes `--prov-map` with `--timing-attr` or a new `--prov`.
- The names an output file carries are unchanged (the hash name is a function of the first-leaf full
  name only, `docs/specs/2026-10-20-5.4-hdl-locations-design.md` H-2, so BLIF bytes do not change).
- **Files that already carry provenance names** (the Norns BLIFs, written with `--name-style
  provenance`): the Norns round-4 lane had to rebuild a name map itself. With version 2 the `O`
  line's `full` field is the H-2 name `hier/short@file:line`, which is the provenance-style name of
  the same net; a consumer that holds a provenance-named BLIF joins it to the map of a `--name-style
  hash --prov-map` run of the same script **by the `full` field** (the netlist structure and order do
  not depend on the name style, section 6). Task 5 adds a test that the `full` of every `O` line
  equals the name `--name-style provenance` writes for the same net.
- `names --map F --lookup SHORT` prints the `O` line, then each of its records and its construct
  chain, indented; `--chain SHORT` prints the chain only; `names --check` verifies the version 2
  invariants (every `r`, `c`, `f` and `t` reference resolves, parents before children, the hash of
  every `O`, no cycle). Python: `Node.provenance()`, `Net.provenance()` over the ABI v7 accessors
  (4.3) and `odin3.read_name_map(path)` (a file reader that does not restore anything into the IR).

## 4. The AST layer: how the chain is obtained

Peter: the AST and the netlist are both provenance. So the AST layer is not a lookup at write time
that may fail; it is held by the design and exported. Three forms, one schema (`C` lines):

- **refs (recommended).** A design-held *construct table*: the AST nodes that any SOURCE record of
  the run references, plus all their ancestors up to the `module`, with kind, level, span, name and
  parent. Built once, when elaboration ends (`odin3_elab_stage2` success), from the elaborated
  store while it is still alive, whatever `--keep-ast` says.
- **constructs (minimal alternative).** The same table restricted to nodes whose kind is a
  construct kind (levels `module` to `stmt`, section 2): no `expr` level; a record that references
  an expression resolves to its nearest construct ancestor.
- **full (dearer alternative).** Every node of the elaborated store (all 68 kinds), parent, span,
  name. For a consumer that wants expression trees; not needed for the chain.

### 4.1 Evidence for choosing a held table over "the AST at write time" (option A)

Option A reads the held AST store when a writer runs. It fails the requirement in four checked
ways: (1) the store is dropped at the end of the read unless `--keep-ast` (1.4), and neither the
Python API, nor `odin3-flow`, nor the CLI default keeps it; (2) the ABI does not expose the
`ast` field, so a Python consumer could not resolve it even if the store were held (1.3); (3) a
design read back from BLIF or JSON never had the store; (4) the construct chain must survive the
file (the map). Making every flow keep the AST costs about 41 bytes per node
(`docs/specs/2026-10-11-2D-elaboration-design.md` section 11, `ODIN3_ELAB_MAX_ELAB_NODES`) plus the
parent index, held for the whole run, to export a subset at the end. Option A stays valid as a
fallback: a writer that finds the elaborated store held may export from it (`ast=full`).

The table is the compact, persisted form of exactly what option A reads, built early. Option B as
originally framed (a construct index field in `odin3_prov_record`) would put the construct in the
hash-consing identity of every SOURCE record and change a struct that IR-12 and check rule 7 test;
rejected: the record already has `ast`, the table resolves it.

### 4.2 Modes are read-time

The mode is a **read-time option**: `read_verilog --prov-ast refs|constructs|full|off` (default
`refs`; the slang reader takes the same option through the shared hook, `src/api/elab_abi.c:79`).
`off` skips the snapshot completely (the read-time off switch: no table, no cost; the map then has
`ast=none`, the `R` lines have empty `cons`, and `check` rule 12 is vacuous). The export option
`--prov-map [refs|constructs|full]` only **selects a subset** of what the read kept (`full` kept
allows all three; `refs` kept allows `refs` or `constructs`; `none` allows none). Equal inputs and
equal options give equal bytes on every machine.

**Budget exhaustion is an error.** If the table does not fit the design byte budget
(`src/passes/budget.h`), `read_verilog` fails with `ODIN3_ERR_NO_MEMORY` and a message naming
`--prov-ast constructs` and `--prov-ast off`. There is no silent downgrade at run time (it would
make `ast=` and the bytes depend on the machine).

### 4.3 The IR and ABI change (recommended form), named exactly

All additive to the IR; no field of `odin3_prov_record`, `odin3_srcloc`, node, net, wire or pin
changes; no ID, no attribute, no op-registry entry changes; the hash-consing identity is unchanged
(`ast` already is part of a SOURCE record's identity, `docs/IR.md` IR-13).

1. **Op-class table** (Task 2; 3.1): `src/ir/opclass.{h,c}`, one design pointer, the
   `odin3_prov_begin_op_class` call, the migration of the sites listed in 3.1, `check` rule 13.
2. **Construct table** (Task 3): `src/ir/construct.h` and `construct.c`: `odin3_construct_table`, a
   design-global store of rows `{ run, ast, part (u8: 0 node, 1 then, 2 else), call (row index of
   the call-site row, 0 none), level (u8), kind (u8), parent (row index + 1, 0 none), file (strtab
   ID), line, col, end_line, end_col, name (strtab ID) }`, 44 bytes a row (measured sizes in
   section 5 use 48 with alignment), a `u64map` from `(run << 32 | ast)` to a chain of rows with
   that node (parts and call instances), rows appended parents first. Functions
   `odin3_construct_snapshot(design, run, mode)`, `odin3_construct_find(design, run, ast, part,
   call)`, `odin3_construct_get(design, index)`, `odin3_construct_end(design)`,

     with `run == this run`, `odin3_prov_end`/`odin3_prov_get`);
   - builds the rows from a **root-down walk with true child edges** (`odin3_ast_walk` or the
     cursor, `odin3_ast_child` by slot, `src/ast/ast.h`), not from `odin3_ast_parent_index_build`,

     that matches no row, or matches more than one with equal spans, is left as a span-only leaf
     (`chain=span`). One body inlined twice therefore has two chains that meet at the callers' blocks
     (or at the module). The cost is one row chain per distinct call site that a record references.

   the true parent agree on every node, so this change guards against a failure that none of the
   test designs triggers; the walk costs the same order as the index.

   Rules for all of them:
   - **Strings** (`file`, `name`, `hier`) are owned by the design, NUL-terminated, and valid until the
     next IR mutation of the design or its destruction, as the header's "Strings" rule says
     (`odin3.h:134-137`); a copied struct holds nothing else. Record IDs and construct rows are
     stable until the design is destroyed (records are never deleted, `prov.h:19-23`; the table is
     append-only and compaction does not touch it).
   - **Ownership**: the caller owns the `out` structs; nothing is allocated for the caller; a
     visitor runs after the walk (as `odin3_prov_visit_sources` does) and may call reader functions;
     it must not change the design.
   - **Dead objects**: a node, net or wire that is dead but not yet compacted answers like a live
     one (as `odin3_prov_visit_sources`, `odin3.h:812`); an ID `compact` has freed names no object and
     is `ODIN3_ERR_INVALID_ARG` (its history is reachable by record ID from a record it was found
     by). An object without provenance gives record 0 from `odin3_obj_get_prov` and an empty
     visit.

     visits, for each SOURCE leaf in the depth-first parent order of `odin3_prov_visit_sources`
     (each record once), the leaf's construct and then its ancestors up to the `module`; **each
     construct is reported once**, at its first reach, with the record of the leaf that reached it.
     A leaf without a construct is skipped. **`odin3_prov_visit_joined`** visits the rows of `J`
     in ascending row order (at most 16, with `n_joined` the total).
   - **Failures**: `ODIN3_ERR_INVALID_ARG` (a NULL pointer or visit, an index or record out of
     range, a kind out of range); `ODIN3_ERR_NO_MEMORY` before any call; both as the conventions say.
   - **dir, level and join reach Python** through `odin3_prov_get_info`, which returns the values
     the exporter uses (the join and the instance rule are computed in C, once, by
     `src/backends/common/prov_export.c`, which the ABI call and the writers share). The Python

     `odin3_prov_get_info`, `odin3_prov_visit_parents`, and `Node.chain()` from

   - `odin3_source` and `odin3_prov_visit_sources` stay as they are (Loki's tests that pin them
     stay valid).

### 4.4 What the chain cannot give

Records with no `ast` (restored locations, IMPORTED) have no construct; their `cons` is empty and
`flags` carries `chain=span`. Objects of a design read from BLIF have `I` records and no chain at
all; that is stated in the map and not guessed. An unrolled loop's iterations share their body's
records (EL-45), so the construct of an iteration is the loop body, with the loop index only in
`hier` when the scope is named. A design read back from a version 2 map has no chain either
(decision 7 of D14).

## 5. Size (measured)

### 5.1 Bounds

- Records are hash-consed, so a record is written once however many objects carry it; `O` and `N`
  lines carry only the record IDs. Records written are the records reachable from the objects
  written (not the whole store); the sizes below count the whole store, so they are upper bounds.
- Construct rows are written once, only for constructs a written record references (`refs`), their
  ancestors included.
- Joins list at most 16 constructs; the ABC budget bounds DERIVED edges by
  `16 x (old cells + new objects) + 65,536` per run (`docs/IR.md` "Provenance across ABC").
- In memory: the construct table is 48 bytes a row (44 plus alignment); the op-class table is at
  most 12 bytes per operation.

### 5.3 Row sizes, map size and time

Row sizes come from formatting every record as its `R` line and every closure node as its `C` line
in the draft schema (ids as decimal, `f1` for the file, the record's `hier` as a flag, `c<ast>`

length of that text. They exclude `cons` lists and the join flags of records that are joins (add
about 6 bytes per member of each `J`), the `T` and `F` lines, and the `recs` and `level` fields of

## 6. Steps after Odin III

A tool, `tools/flow/prov_post.py` (standard library only, next to `timing_attr.py`, installed by
the same `CMakeLists.txt`, `pyproject.toml` and `plugins/python/entry.py` rows as V1.1.1), command
`odin3-prov-post FLOW_DIR`. It reads:

- the BLIF VPR read (hash names) and its map (`<blif>.names`, version 2);
- VPR's packed netlist `.net` (XML; block tree with the primitive, BLE, CLB nesting and the atom
  name of each primitive) and `.place` (block, x, y, subtile).

It appends to the map, in the same file and schema: `R` lines of level `ble` and `clb`
(`dir=coarsen`, `step` `t<n>.0` a new `T` row `pack`, parents = the records of the atoms inside),
`R` lines of level `site` (`dir=rewrite`, parent the CLB, the `.place` coordinates in `flags`),
and `A` lines keyed by atom name. Joins use section 3.3 unchanged: a CLB whose atoms blend
distinct anchors is labelled a join by `pack`. The map is rewritten atomically (temporary file
then `os.replace`). It parses version 2 with its own stdlib parser (`tools/flow/name_map_v2.py`,
Task 7): `tools/flow` is stdlib-only and `odin3.read_name_map` needs the cffi package.

**Atom name join.** VPR names a block after the net its first output drives and an I/O pad after
its port (`docs/V1.1.1.md` Join). With `--name-style hash` those names are the hash names written
in the BLIF; the `O` lines of the map are keyed by the same short names, so the join is exact and
needs no second BLIF. A BLIF written with short names has no map keyed by them, so the tool
refuses it (the V1.1.1 route with two BLIFs stays for that case). A provenance-named BLIF joins by
the `full` field (3.5).

## 7. Consumers (read-only study; no consumer code in this lane)

### 7.1 Loki (how it labels cells from provenance today)

Loki (`loki/` repo, Python, imports `odin3`) labels final cells by a **span join**:

- A word-level snapshot is taken after `proc; opt; flatten; ...` but before `lower`
  (`loki/snapshot.py:39-62`), one `WordCell` per live word node with `spans=node_spans(node)`, where
  `node_spans` is `node.sources()` (`loki/snapshot.py:20-21`), i.e. `odin3_prov_visit_sources`
  (`plugins/python/odin3.py:650`): file, line, column, end line, end column.
- Final cells (LUTs, flip-flops, gates) get the same `spans` from the final design
  (`loki/extract.py:51`).
- `match_words` (`loki/labels.py:41`) says a word cell W matches a final cell c when `set(W.spans)`
  is a non-empty subset of `set(c.spans)`, sorted by span count then ID. `_cell_label`
  (`loki/labels.py:161`) groups matches with equal (spans, type) (`_groups`, `:75`), sets
  `construct_types` (distinct types joined by `|`), `construct_type` (`_primary`, `:147`),
  `n_construct_cells`, `ambiguous` (identical spans cannot be told apart), and the role.
- The module path cannot come from the record. Flatten keeps the instance path in the record's
  `hier` (`src/passes/flatten/prov.c:33-62`), but the ABI does not return `hier`, and a cell lists
  only the innermost instantiation span. `module_path` (`loki/labels.py:83`) therefore climbs
  from the innermost site to the top through the module hierarchy it recorded before `flatten`
  (`take_instances`, `loki/snapshot.py:65`) and writes `top/*` when a module has several parents
  (the open question in `docs/specs/2026-10-21-l0-dataset-design.md:124-129`).
- The spec (`docs/specs/2026-10-21-l0-dataset-design.md:102-151`) states what the labels rest on:
  `sources()` "is the only provenance the ABI exposes" (`:108-111`); a LUT lists all spans of its
  cone (`:150-151`, section 5.3); a LUT that merges several constructs lists all of them and "a LUT
  is not split".

What D14 changes for Loki, concretely:

1. **The join becomes a walk.** With records and the chain exposed (Tasks 3 and 6), the construct
   of a final cell is read from its record ancestry, not by span-subset matching. The
   identical-span ambiguity (`ambiguous`) goes away **for expression cells and instances**: two
   instances of one module have different context chains, and two statements on one line have
   different constructs. It does not go away for cells made by one `proc` switch: the muxes and
   selects of a switch share one record (`docs/specs/2026-10-11-2E-proc-design.md:898-906`), so
   Loki word cells made by one switch still tie, and `ambiguous` stays for them.
2. **`module_path` is exact** from the flatten records' context chain, including nested instances.
3. **New label columns** become possible from the chain: `block` (always, assign, generate),
   `branch` (the if or case arm), `stmt`, `expr` kind and span, `join_of` (the constructs a LUT
   blends, from section 3.3) and `n_constructs`. `LABEL_COLUMNS` (`loki/schemas.py:21`) is a Loki
   schema change in its own lane; the existing columns keep their meaning.
4. **L15 loss audit** (`work/controller-scripts/ROUND4-PLAN.md`, Loki r5: "construct at file:line
   lost at pass P because merged with Q") needs the join label of 3.3: the first `coarsen` record
   in a construct's descendants that has `join` and names the construct in `cons` is "lost at step
   P" (pass, run, op), "joined with Q" is the other members of `cons`. Today this has to be
   reconstructed from pass-by-pass snapshots of spans; with the map it is a lookup per construct,
   and the snapshots become a check.
5. **From-BLIF designs** (`docs/specs/2026-10-21-from-blif-design.md`): a design read back from a
   BLIF carries no chain (decision 7 of D14; the map is not restored into the IR). Loki can read
   the version 2 map file of the flow that produced the BLIF directly (`odin3.read_name_map`,
   keyed by the BLIF's own names) and take the chain from the file. A BLIF without a map has none.

Loki's stated facts (`docs/specs/2026-10-21-l0-dataset-design.md` section 5.1, re-checked by
its tests) stay true: `Node.sources()` and `odin3_prov_visit_sources` do not change (Task 3).
Loki keeps its span join as the fallback for designs without a map (BLIF only, no Odin III
provenance) and for the `chain=span` records of 4.4.

## 8. Compatibility

- `--name-style short|provenance|hash` unchanged. Without `--prov-map`, `hash` writes the map
  version 1 byte-identical to today; the netlist files are byte-identical in every mode (the hash
  name depends only on the first-leaf full name, H-2).
- `--prov-map` without `--name-style hash` is an error (3.5). `write_blif` PLAIN, `write_json` and
  Verilog with short or provenance names carry no chain.
- `read_blif --name-map`, `read_json --name-map` and `read_verilog -netlist -name-map`
  (`src/frontends/verilog/read_verilog.c:446`) read version 1 as now; they accept version 2 and use
  only the `O` lines (ignoring other types with one WARNING per type). **None of them restores
  records, parents or constructs**: a design read back from a version 2 map has restored
  locations (SOURCE with `ast` 0) and no chain, and writing it out again produces a map without
  `R`, `C`, `T` lines. This is stated, not hidden (question 4 offers a restore task). A version 1-only
  build refuses a version 2 file at the header; this is why version 2 is opt-in.
- `names --lookup/--reverse/--check` accept both versions; version 1 output is unchanged.
- Goldens: none changes. The goldens are BLIF, logs and `.prov` produced without the new option
  (`CLAUDE.md` rule 9); the new code runs only on request, the construct table is not written to
  any existing output, and `dump_locs` is untouched. Task 4 adds a test that runs the whole micro
  set before and after and compares every writer output byte for byte.
- **ABI version 6 to 7.** A v6 plugin does **not** load against v7: `src/api/plugin.c:30` passes
  `ODIN3_ABI_VERSION` to the plugin's init, `plugins/example_c/example_plugin.c:108` rejects any
  other value (`ODIN3_ERR_ABI_MISMATCH`), and `plugins/python/odin3.py:115` requires an exact match
  with the library. The consequence is that every compiled plugin is rebuilt against the v7 header
  (in this tree: `plugins/example_c` and the `tests/unit` plugins, which are built with the tree);
  third-party plugins must be rebuilt. The `odin3.h:57` comment ("incremented on every incompatible
  change") is the stated policy, but v5 (S1 simulation API) and v6 (the reader seam of D13) were
  bumped for additive changes (`docs/V1.1.0.md:44-45`), so the practice is a bump for every API
  addition and there is no stability promise (`docs/V1.1.0.md:92`). The alternative is an additive
  route without a bump (the functions are added and the version stays 6, so old plugins keep
  loading, but a v6 host with a new header would fail only at call time); this is question 2.
- `schema_version`: the map's header version is the schema version (2); each later additive
  field gets a new trailing field, and readers must tolerate unknown trailing fields on `R` and `C`
  lines.
- Memory: the construct table adds 48 bytes per row to every `read_verilog` run, counted in the
  design byte budget (`src/passes/budget.h`; section 5.3 gives the shift of the opt and budget
  gates, `src/passes/opt/register.c:238`, `src/passes/budget.c:154`). If the table does not fit,
  the read fails with `ODIN3_ERR_NO_MEMORY` and names `--prov-ast constructs|off` (4.2).

## 10. Open questions for Peter

Each has a recommendation, consistent with the body above.

1. **AST layer form.** (a) Always-on construct table versus built on request. Recommendation: the
   `refs` table, built at the end of every read, with the read-time switch `--prov-ast off` and the
   Task 4 time/RSS gate (section 5.3) deciding the CLI default; minimal alternative `constructs`
   (no `expr` level), dearer alternative `full`. Is `refs` the default?
2. **ABI v7 and v6 plugins.** (b) Recommendation: bump to v7 and say the consequence plainly:
   every compiled plugin is rebuilt (the loader and the Python binding need an exact match). It
   follows the practice of v5 and v6 and the "no stability promise" of v1.1.0. The alternative is an
   additive route that keeps the version at 6 (old plugins load; no accessor guard). Agree to the
   IR/ABI additions (two design-global tables, ABI v7, check rules 12 and 13, no record field)?
3. **Opt-in and hash names.** (c) Recommendation: `--prov-map` is opt-in, requires `--name-style
   hash` (an error otherwise), and `odin3-flow --timing-attr` and `--prov` turn it on; version 2
   maps are refused by version 1 builds. A provenance-named BLIF (Norns) joins by the `full` field.
   Or make version 2 the default (changes every existing map file and its goldens)?
4. **Read-back of version 2 maps.** (d) Recommendation: not in this patch. `read_blif`,
   `read_json` and `read_verilog -netlist` keep reading the `O` lines only; the chain is lost on a
   write, read, write cycle and the map says so; consumers read the map file. The alternative is a
   task that restores records and the construct table from `R`/`C` lines with a write-read-write
   round-trip test; it would give SOURCE records an `ast` that does not exist in any store, which
   needs its own design. Agree?
5. **Distinct construct, join rule, proc arms.** (e) Recommendation: the rule of 3.3: distinctness at
   the nearest `stmt`-or-coarser ancestor, the instance and context parents out of `J` and the
   instance join computed from context chains, one cap rule (16 members, `lossy=cap`,
   `lossy=bulk` declared by the pass). **`proc` mux arms are `refine`, not a join** (a mux is the
   hardware form of one `if`/`case` statement); the alternative labels a mux a join of its arm
   statements. Which?
6. **Version doc.** Recommendation: a minor, `docs/V1.2.0.md` (renamed from this working file when
   D14 is accepted). Reason: the change breaks compiled v6 plugins (question 2), and D13 shipped ABI
   v6 with the v1.1.0 minor; the earlier "patch" argument was weak because v1.0.3's ABI v5 never
   shipped alone, it rolled into v1.1.0 (`docs/V1.1.0.md:31,44`). Or keep the patch doc
   `docs/V1.1.2.md` and note the rebuild in the release notes?
7. **Post-Odin tool placement.** Recommendation: `tools/flow/prov_post.py` (stdlib, with
   `timing_attr`), keyed by the atom names of the hash-name BLIF, with its own stdlib v2 parser
   (Task 7). Or in the Python package (needs cffi)?
8. **VPR check.** Recommendation: it is done (section 6.1: identical `.net`, `.place`, `.route`,
   critical path and wirelength on three designs); repeat on `sha` and `diffeq1` at Task 8 through
   heavy-run when lanes are idle; no second VPR-only BLIF is needed unless that fails. OK?
9. **Netlist-only inputs.** Recommendation: designs read from BLIF or JSON without Odin III records
   export no chain (`chain=span` or none) and say so; no attempt to parse source files to
   reconstruct constructs. OK?
10. **Consumer sequence.** Recommendation: the attribution drop of `prov.blif` first (Task 7,
    smallest, in this repo), then Loki labels and L15, then Norns N17, then the Wyrd view; Loki r5
    waits for Task 6.
11. **Scope of languages.** Recommendation: Verilog and SystemVerilog (slang) are in, through

12. **Dropping the `design` and `bit` levels.** (f) Recommendation: drop both (no record or AST node
    sits at either; `lower` records are per word cell). The level list in D14 and section 2 is then
    module, block, branch, stmt, expr, word, gate, lut, ble, clb, site, with the instance as a
    separate axis. Agree?
